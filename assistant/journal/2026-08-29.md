**Thoughts on best practices and where this could go:**

The persona split (PM tracks schedule, professor tracks rigor, editor tracks
communication) is essentially structured, role-separated review — the same idea behind
having distinct reviewers for different concerns in software engineering, or RACI-style
authority separation in project management. There's a genuine parallel worth noting: this
capstone is *studying* exactly that kind of authority/responsibility separation in the
NAS (RACCI matrices, decision-authority boundaries), and the assistant scaffold now models
a small version of the same idea for itself — distinct personas with distinct authorities
and objectives, rather than one generic assistant blending every concern into one voice.
That's a nice throughline, not a forced one.

That said, this whole pattern (personas + journal + scheduled tag-ups for an AI
collaborator) is an experiment, not an established methodology — it should earn its keep
by actually improving the capstone's pace/rigor, not by being clever for its own sake.
Concrete risks to watch for as it's adapted: persona proliferation (adding a new persona
for every review angle until sessions are mostly persona overhead); running personas
reflexively instead of when their specific lens is actually needed; and letting the
journal/tag-up ritual become busywork that generates logs nobody reads back. The
Professor persona itself would probably flag that risk if asked — worth actually asking
it, periodically, whether the scaffold is still earning its keep.

Future extensions worth considering once the base pattern proves out: a "Stakeholder"
persona for red-teaming the architecture from a specific NAS actor's perspective (airline
ops, ATC, passenger) once §7-§8 are further along; mining the journal at project's end for
the report's own methodology/process narrative; and treating `session-wrap-up` and
`session-tagup` as complementary bookends (tag-up opens a session with a status read,
wrap-up closes it with a formal record) rather than redundant with each other.
