# Claims

A claim note captures something important, contestable, reusable, or evidence-dependent —
not every sentence in the project earns one. Each should contain: the claim, scope and
qualifications, supporting evidence, contradicting evidence, your confidence, your
interpretation, related claims, practical implications, and open questions.

## Empty for now — on purpose

This folder starts empty rather than pre-filled. The 2026-09-05 reorg's retrieval-baseline
test surfaced one strong candidate: the tension between individual-aircraft trajectory
optimization and sector-level throughput/controller workload, already named in
`../models/stakeholder-personas.md` (the ATC controller and airline dispatcher personas)
and slated as a `to-do-list.md` §9 checklist item ("individual optimal trajectory vs.
network congestion").

Per this workspace's protection of cognitive value: don't let an AI pre-draft that claim
note wholesale. Before it gets written, state the claim in your own words first — what do
*you* think the tension actually is, and why does it matter to this project? Prompts worth
using to get started: "Explain this conflict in your own words," "What evidence would
change your mind about which side wins in a given scenario?," "How does this relate to
D-002's decomposition choice?" Use your answer as the note's nucleus; AI assistance can
then help structure, cross-link, and cite evidence around it, clearly distinguished from
your own wording.

## When to add another

Create a claim note when something meets at least one of: it's genuinely contestable (a
reasonable person could disagree), it's reusable across more than one section of the
project, or a real decision depends on which way it resolves. If in doubt, it probably
belongs in `../questions/open-questions.md` instead, until it's specific enough to become
a claim.
