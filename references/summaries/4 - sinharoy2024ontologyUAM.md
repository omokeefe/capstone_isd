## What it is

Proposes a structured framework for elaborating Urban Air Mobility (UAM) architectures by
combining ontology (to unify understanding of systems, relationships, and processes across
a system-of-systems) with a reference-model-based MBSE approach, so that metro-area-specific
architectures can be rapidly derived from a common baseline rather than built ad hoc each
time.

## Why it's valuable — and to what

- Literature review section: none directly / MBSE methodology background, but closely
  analogous domain (an aviation SoS being architected from first principles).
- Decomposition / architecture (§6, §10): this is arguably the **closest methodological
  precedent** in the whole reference set to what this capstone is attempting — an
  ontology-first, MBSE-second approach to architecting an aviation system-of-systems with
  multiple regulators, service providers, and operators. Directly informs how to structure
  the domain decomposition and avoid "ad hoc" architecture per their stated motivation.
- Stakeholder / objective ontology (§7-§9): UAM stakeholder set (regulators, PSUs, fleet
  operators, vertiport operators) is a smaller-scale analog to the NAS PESTLE inventory in
  `assistant/memory/stakeholder-register.md` — useful as a cross-check pattern.
  DeLaurentis (last author here) is also the author of `delaurentis2005sosTransportation`,
  the foundational transportation-as-SoS paper already in this register — same research
  lineage.
- Glossary / terminology: UAM, PSU (Provider of Services for UAM), UML (UAM Maturity Level).
