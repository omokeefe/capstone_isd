## What it is

The 82-page ICAO vision document for a global, interoperable ATM system (planning horizon "up to and
beyond 2025", baseline the 2000 environment). It is explicitly a statement of *what* is envisaged, not
*how*: technology-independent, sitting above ConOps, standards and plans in the document hierarchy.
Chapter 2 defines eight ATM operational concept components (airspace organization and management,
aerodrome operations, demand and capacity balancing, traffic synchronization, airspace user
operations, conflict management, ATM service delivery management, information services). Appendices
cover the ATM community, expectations, benefits, performance, evolution and examples.

## Why it's valuable — and to what

- Literature review section: `to-do-list.md` §4 (flight execution) as the concept-level anchor
  for how nominal ATM is supposed to work; also useful for §5 (ConOps) framing.
- Decomposition / architecture (§6, §10): **strong.** A standing, authoritative functional decomposition
  of ATM (the eight components) and the "ATM community" actor set (Appendix A); it defines the
  ATM system as "the collaborative integration of humans, information, technology, facilities and
  services." A ready-made reference to compare the SysML decomposition against.
- Trajectory-intent chain: ATM acts "directly on the flight trajectory" of a manned or unmanned
  vehicle in all phases; **management by trajectory**, dynamic 4-D trajectory control, and an
  agreed trajectory with "minimal deviation from the user-requested flight trajectory." That is the
  project's central concept at ICAO level. The airspace user operations component (mission
  planning as a collaborative exercise; operational control exercised by the airspace user) speaks
  directly to the airline-side end of the chain.
- Stakeholder / objective ontology (§7-§9): the vision statement (safe, optimum economic operations,
  environmentally sustainable, national security) and the expectations/performance appendices give a
  neutral objective set for the §8 categories; the "differing performance demands" of community
  members are the seed of §9 conflicts. Collaborative decision making and the "equity and access"
  principle are relevant to balancing individual vs. system optima.
- Optimization study (§11-§13): demand and capacity balancing (§2.4) frames the DCB/TFM decision
  support the optimization work would sit inside.
- Glossary / terminology: ATM community, ATM system, operational concept, management by
  trajectory, conflict horizon, operational control, 4-D trajectory, demand-capacity balancing;
  Appendix B is a ready-made glossary.
