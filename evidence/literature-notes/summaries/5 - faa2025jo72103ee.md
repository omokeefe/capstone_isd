## What it is

The FAA's order for operating and managing air traffic facilities: the management and administrative companion to JO 7110.65BB (`faa2025jo711065bb`). 7110.65 tells a controller how to control traffic. 7210.3 tells facilities (ARTCCs, terminals, FSSs) and the traffic management system how to be organized, staffed, coordinated, and logged. It has seven parts: 1 Basic (administration, equipment, LOAs, special flights), 2 ARTCCs, 3 Terminal, 4 FSS, **5 Traffic Management System (Ch. 18)**, 6 Regulatory (waivers, TFRs), 7 System Operations Security. This copy is the consolidated edition with Changes 1–3 incorporated, so it is current as of 2026-07-09.

## Why it's valuable — and to what

Chapter 18 is the FAA primary source for **how traffic flow management (TFM) works as an organization**, which the project was missing. It closes the gap the 09-26 BB pass and the 09-27 journal identified: *"The AOC ↔ ATCSCC exchange … stays B/C until JO 7210.3 or FAA CDM/CTOP material is registered."*

- **Literature review:** `to-do-list.md` §4 (nominal ATC/flight execution, TFM side) and the disruption material in §3–§4 (GDP, ground stops, diversion recovery, severe weather). Pairs with `faa2025jo711065bb` as the FAA-side primary source set.
- **Decomposition / architecture (§6, §10):** the TFM hierarchy and its links.
  - **Organizations and authority:** ATCSCC "delegated the authority to direct the operation of the TM system" and is the "final approving authority regarding all interfacility TM initiatives" (¶18-2-3, ¶18-2-3i). TMUs exist at ARTCCs and designated terminals and "direct traffic flows … in conjunction with, or as directed by the ATCSCC" (¶18-2-4). TMUs keep latitude for local tactical adjustments such as sector-to-sector MIT and fix balancing (¶18-2-3 NOTE).
  - **Hierarchy path:** tower → TRACON/terminal TMU → ARTCC TMU → ATCSCC. The ARTCC TMU is the "focal point" and coordinates with the ATCSCC on behalf of the TRACON or tower (¶18-2-4c NOTE, ¶18-5-4d, ¶18-5-7a). N90 is the exception and goes direct.
  - **Positions (line of authority, §18-3):** ATCSCC NOM → NTMO → NTMS; ARTCC TMO → STMC → TMC; terminal TMO → STMC/TMCIC → TMC, with the OSIC performing TM where no TMC exists.
  - **Tools and systems named:** NTML (the log/coordination tool), TFMS (monitor/alert, TSD, RAD, PDRR/ABRR), FSM/FSA, TBFM, TFDM, ERAM, CCSD/WSD, DRT, OIS, SEC website, NADIN.
- **Stakeholder / objective ontology (§7-§9):** the TM mission gives an authoritative objective statement: "balance air traffic demand with system capacity to ensure the maximum efficient utilization of the NAS" (¶18-1-1). The policies are useful for objective classification: "least restrictive methods" (¶18-7-3), "Any TMI creates an impact on customers" (¶18-7-1b), "equitable assignment of delays to all system users" (¶18-10-2), and TBO/TBM prioritized over other methods (¶18-6-2, ¶18-6-4). The customer role is specified (FEA/FCA "Customers are expected to…", ¶18-8-3), which gives the RACCI an FAA-defined operator responsibility set.
- **Optimization study (§11-§13):** CTOP (¶18-12-1) is an FAA-operated allocation algorithm over operator-ranked trajectory options (TOS). It is a ready example of a decision-support capability *inside* the architecture, not a standalone optimization problem. GDP slot substitution (¶18-10-12) and diversion recovery priority (¶18-4-5) are other collaborative allocation mechanisms.
- **Glossary / terminology:** TMI (with a typed list: altitude/capping/tunneling/LAADR, MIT, MINIT, fix balancing, airborne holding, DSP, TFMS programs, reroutes, GS; ¶18-7-4), TBO/TBM (¶18-6-1, ¶18-6-3; TBO trajectory = lat/lon/alt/time), FEA/FCA/EI/ICR/dynamic list (¶18-8-2), MAP (¶18-9-1), GDP/DAS/GAAP/UDP (¶18-10-4), CTOP/TOS (¶18-12-3), NTML, SIR, Operations Plan / Advance Plan / trigger (¶18-21-2), Tactical Customer Advocate (TCA).
- **Other:** the trajectory-intent chain. ¶18-6-1 defines TBO as managing flights "using time-based management … information exchanged between air and ground systems, and the aircraft's ability to fly trajectories in time and space." This is the FAA's own statement of the intent → trajectory link the capstone is centered on, and it is the US counterpart to the GreAT/SESAR TBO sources.

## Key evidence for open items (grade A: what the order itself says)

**1. AOC (system user) ↔ ATCSCC exchange: now primary-sourced.** The order describes flows running directly between the operator and the ATCSCC/TFMS, not through tactical ATC:

| Flow | Direction | ¶ |
|---|---|---|
| EDCTs transmitted "to ARTCCs and linked system users" | ATCSCC → operator (and → ARTCC) | 18-10-6e |
| GDP/CTOP/GS/reroute advisories to "air traffic control facilities and user groups" | ATCSCC → operator | 18-10-6c, 18-12-4, 18-15-2a7 |
| Schedule changes submitted to FSM | operator → ATCSCC (FSM) | 18-10-3 |
| GDP user options (intermediate landing, CTA substitution): "Users must coordinate options directly with the ATCSCC" | operator ↔ ATCSCC | 18-10-12 |
| Trajectory Options Set: "A message sent by participating flight operators to TFMS" | operator → TFMS | 18-7-4h6, 18-12-3b |
| Early Intent route preferences to TFMS; FCA name in flight plan remarks; routing decisions or coordination with the ATCSCC Tactical Customer Advocate | operator → TFMS / ATCSCC | 18-8-2c, 18-8-3 |
| Diversion recovery priorities via the DRT or "DVRSN" in remarks | operator → ATCSCC → ARTCC → tower/TRACON | 18-4-5 |
| Hotlines/telcons: capacity issues, mitigation, routes, weather, outages, "customer preferences for initiatives and alternatives" | ATCSCC ↔ operators (customers may be listen-only) | 18-4-1d |
| Operations Plan / Planning Webinar with "airline strategic planners" and "customer weather forecasters" | operator ↔ ATCSCC Planning Team | 18-21-2a, 18-21-4a |

The order calls the operator "system users," "customers," "flight operators," or "airline planners." It never says "AOC" or "dispatch." (The one "AOC" in the order is NOAA's Aircraft Operations Center, an unrelated acronym collision.) The mapping of "system user / customer" to the model's `airlineOperationsCenter` (via the ATC coordinator role) is therefore still an abstraction that has to be recorded. Seamster and Berry & Pace (grade B) support it.

**2. The 09-27 pasted TMU → ATCSCC note: mostly verified, with corrections.**

| Claim in the note | Verdict | ¶ |
|---|---|---|
| TMUs notify the ATCSCC of component changes (runway/NAVAID/radar shutdowns, TELCO, computer outages, procedural changes) as soon as aware | **Confirmed** (by "telephone or hotline coordination") | 18-2-4a6 + NOTE; 18-5-4c1, c3 |
| Exchange via NTML, TFMS, and telcons | **Confirmed**; NTML is to be used "in preference to other methods" | 18-5-3, 18-5-8b, 18-4-1 |
| TMIs expected to cause reportable delay of 15+ min go through the ATCSCC | **Confirmed** (15 min per JO 7210.55) | 18-7-7a |
| Local ground stop: notify the ATCSCC before, or as soon as practical after | **Confirmed**; local GS ≤ 30 min, no extension without ATCSCC approval | 18-13-3 |
| MIT restrictions submitted to or updated with the ATCSCC | **Confirmed with nuance:** ≤ 10 MIT is coordinated between facilities; ≥ 25 MIT needs a shared FEA; ≥ 40 MIT needs a conference | 18-7-12, -13 |
| TBFM/TBM status reported to the ATCSCC | **Confirmed** | 18-5-4c1, 18-6-9b, 18-6-11 |
| FEA/FCA shared with the ATCSCC | **Confirmed** | 18-8-4a3, 18-7-13 |
| TMU "coordinates EDCTs" | **Correction:** the ATCSCC issues EDCTs. The ARTCC TMU assigns from the DAS table in some modes, relays EDCT issues, and requests revised EDCTs from the ATCSCC | 18-10-6e, 18-10-7 |
| TMU sends TOS / Route Amendment Dialog reroute requests | **Correction:** TOS is sent **by flight operators** to TFMS, not by TMUs. RAD is the ARTCC TMC's tool for airborne reroutes sent **to ERAM** for sector controllers. The TMU→ATCSCC reroute request goes through NTML | 18-7-4h5–6, 18-5-11 |
| MAP red/yellow alerts over a 1.5–2.5 h look-ahead are "uploaded" to the ATCSCC | **Partly wrong:** the 1.5–2.5 h look-ahead is confirmed, but alerts are handled *within* the facility (TMU notifies the affected area). What goes to the ATCSCC is any **adjustment of MAP values** | 18-9-2b, 18-9-3c–d |
| Delay reports | **Confirmed:** verbal notification at 15 min, NTML updates, verbal at 90 min, TMO notice at 45 min; EDCT delays from GDP/AFP/CTOP/GS excepted | 18-5-12 |
| Copies of all formal interfacility TM agreements to the ATCSCC | **Confirmed** | 18-2-4a3 |
| SWAP | **Exists** (§18-16); severe weather management is ATCSCC-led | 18-15, 18-16 |

**3. Air carrier → ARTCC flight-plan interface.** §6-5 (Air Carrier Computer Interface Program) lets air carriers file flight plans computer-to-computer via NADIN/Center B. Plans can be filed no earlier than 3 h before departure; changes after filing, and plans inside 45 min, must be telephoned. This is a primary-sourced operator → ATC (ARTCC host) flight-plan flow, distinct from the TFM flows above. ¶18-7-4h2 says operators "should amend flight plans when they are more than 45 minutes from departure."

## Flags

- **Edition:** consolidated Basic + CHG 1–3 (current to 2026-07-09). This is newer and more complete than the BB file (Basic only). When quoting, cite "JO 7210.3EE CHG 3" and check the FAA site for a CHG 4 or a new edition.
- **Not a duplicate** of anything in the register. It is the companion order to `faa2025jo711065bb`. `seamster2011collabSystems` cites the older JO 7210.3W, so its paragraph numbers are not reliable pointers into EE.
- **Terminology gap:** the order never names the AOC, dispatcher, or ATC coordinator ("system users/customers/flight operators" only). Mapping those roles to model parts is an abstraction to document, not something the order states.
- **Reference chain:** several procedures defer to other orders not in the register: JO 7210.55 (delay reporting), JO 7210.637 (TFDM), JO 7210.634 (quality control), JO 7610.14 (special operations), JO 8020.16. None are needed yet.
- **Read depth is partial:** Chapter 18 was read for the TFM questions only. Parts 1–4, 6, and 7 were read from the table of contents, plus §6-5. The BB summary says PIREP dissemination is in "JO 7210.3 ¶6-3-1". That pointer was **not** checked in this edition.
- No digital highlights or annotations in the PDF.
