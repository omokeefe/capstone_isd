## What it is

The Airport Compliance Manual chapter on Grant Assurance 22 (Economic Nondiscrimination). It covers how sponsors of federally obligated airports must make facilities available to aeronautical users on reasonable, non-discriminatory terms. Sections: 9.1 introduction; 9.2 rental fees and charges (including 9.2b signatory vs nonsignatory carriers); 9.3–9.5 charges and tenant terms; 9.6 FBOs and other aeronautical services (including 9.6g proprietary exclusive); 9.7 availability of leased space; 9.8 air carrier airport access; 9.9 civil rights; 9.10 residency preference.

## What the capstone uses it for

The AOC ↔ Airport link in `nas_context_diagram.sysml` (gate assignment, note 1):

- **9.2b:** "The primary obligation of a signatory tenant is to lease space in airport facilities and commit to long-term financial support of the development and operation of the airport." The sponsor "cannot unreasonably deny signatory status to an air carrier willing and able to assume the obligations of a signatory carrier."
- **9.6g, proprietary exclusive:** the sponsor "may itself exercise, but may not grant to another entity, the exclusive right to conduct any aeronautical activity." The owner quoted 9.6g(1): "most sponsors recognize that commercial aeronautical services are best provided by private enterprise. However, there are times an airport will choose to either exercise a full proprietary exclusive or offer some services while allowing competing services." Note that 9.6g covers services such as fueling and servicing, not terminal or gate leases.
- **9.8a:** Grant Assurance 23 "prohibits a sponsor from directly or indirectly conveying an exclusive right to an air carrier." A sponsor "may not deny an air carrier access solely based on the nonavailability of existing facilities" and "must make arrangements for accommodations if reasonably possible." 9.8 names "ticket counter, gate, and ramp space" as the historically constrained facilities.
- **9.8b:** Grant Assurance 39 requires large and medium hubs to report every denial of access (each February and August).
- **Footnote 10 (9.7d):** exclusive-use lease areas on AIP-funded pavement require prior FAA approval.

**Model implication:** the airport sponsor keeps final authority over terminal and gate space. Airlines hold it by lease, not by right, and the sponsor must accommodate other carriers.

## Limits

- **It does not describe preferential-use or common-use gate allocation**, nor who assigns gates day to day. Within this chapter, "preferential" appears only in 9.10, residency-based treatment. Chs. 8 (Exclusive Rights), 12 (Aeronautical Lease Agreements), and 14 (Safety and Efficiency Restrictions) were also checked on 2026-09-28 and don't cover it either. That split stays **[C]** in the model until another source is found, such as an airport use and lease agreement or an ACRP report.
