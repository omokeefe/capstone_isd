## What it is

Applies MBSE to the design of aircraft engine inlets using a structured Requirements-
Functional-Logical-Physical (RFLP) process in MagicDraw/SysML, incorporating real
industry-defined requirements from Collins Aerospace to manage safety, reliability, and
regulatory-compliance constraints.

## Why it's valuable — and to what

- Literature review section: none / MBSE methodology background.
- Decomposition / architecture (§6, §10): the RFLP process (requirements → functional →
  logical → physical) is a clean, citable MBSE technique reference if the capstone wants to
  justify its own modeling approach or discuss alternative MBSE process patterns —
  applicable at the sub-system level rather than SoS level.
- Stakeholder / objective ontology (§7-§9): not directly applicable — its "stakeholders"
  are design-engineering disciplines, not NAS operational stakeholders.
- Glossary / terminology: RFLP, ARCADIA (mentioned as an alternative framework).

## Rating

**2/5** — marginal for this capstone. It's a legitimate MBSE case study, but it's a
component-level design problem (an engine inlet), not a system-of-systems or operations
architecture — limited direct transfer to the NAS/airline-ops storyline beyond "here's what
an RFLP MBSE process looks like."
