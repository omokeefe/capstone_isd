## What it is

A 37-page MDPI review (University of Bradford / Valeo) synthesizing how AI (ML/NLP/LLMs)
and ontology-based knowledge representation can transform traditional Failure Mode and
Effects Analysis (FMEA) into an intelligent, semantically-structured, model-integrated
process. Covers FMEA foundations/limitations, AI techniques for failure
prediction/classification, the shift toward MBSE, function-modeling frameworks
(FBS/SBF/FBSta), ontology types and design methodologies (NeOn, Methontology,
DILIGENT), knowledge graphs, and hybrid AI-ontology-LLM frameworks. Domain focus is
automotive/manufacturing (Valeo-funded), not aviation.

## Why it's valuable — and to what

- Literature review section: none — no aviation content.
- Decomposition / architecture (§6, §10): indirect — its discussion of ontology types
  (upper/domain/task) and design methodologies (NeOn/Methontology/DILIGENT) is a solid
  generic methodological reference if the capstone needs to justify how to structure or
  validate its own objective/cost/value ontology.
- Stakeholder / objective ontology (§7-9): the most relevant hook. Section 2.4 makes an
  explicit, citable point that traditional FMEA is subsystem/component-focused and
  "less effective for evaluating risks at the system-of-systems level" — directly usable
  to motivate why local (component-level) risk/objective analysis fails to capture
  SoS-level or cross-domain conflicts, analogous to the capstone's myopic-optimization
  argument.
- Optimization study (§11-13): none — framed as a design-time reliability practice, not
  an operational optimization method.
- Glossary / terminology: FMEA, FBS (Function-Behaviour-Structure), upper/domain/task
  ontology, knowledge graph (KG), Failure Mode Avoidance (FMA).
