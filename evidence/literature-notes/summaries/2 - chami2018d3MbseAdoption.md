## What it is

A primary organizational/human-factors study on MBSE adoption. Summarizes best practices and a
set of common MBSE adoption challenges (upfront investment, adoption-strategy choice,
purpose/scope definition, change resistance, executive sponsorship, method
definition/customization, modularity/reusability, complexity management, tool dependency, model
visualization), illustrated with industry case studies (e.g., Kongsberg's Joint Strike Missile
project), and proposes a "D3 MBSE Adoption Toolbox" addressing them.

**Page 2**
- **Highlight:** "The MBSE movement was reinforced with the successful adoption of Unified Modeling Language (UML) (OMG 2007) and Model-Driven Architecture (MDA) (OMG 2003). However, UML was too complicated and unnatural for solving systems engineering (SE) domain-specific problems (Morkevicius & Gudas 2011). Therefore, the Object Management Group (OMG) initiated the creation of a domain-specific language and released the first version of SysML (OMG 2012a) in 2007. As SysML is a profile of UML, it has been easily adopted by most UML tool vendors. It is commonly misunderstood in SE that SysML as a modeling language is enough to successfully apply MBSE in the organization. (Silingas & Butleris 2009) clearly state that the modeling language is just the language, and must be combined with a methodology to be useful."
