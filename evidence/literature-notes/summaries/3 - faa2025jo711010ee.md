## What it is

The FAA's procedures and phraseology order for flight service specialists, the people who staff Flight Service Stations (FSS). It is the third member of the 7110/7210 family already in the register: JO 7110.65BB is for controllers, JO 7210.3EE is for facility management and traffic management, and this one is for flight services. Eight chapters: 1 General, 2 General Control, 3 Emergency Services, 4 In-Flight Services, 5 Pre-Flight Services (pilot briefing), **6 Flight Data Services (flight plan handling)**, 7 NOTAM Services, 8 FAA Weather Services. Appendices A–D cover the flight plan forms and message formats.

## Why it's valuable — and to what

The order as a whole is about a service that mostly sits outside the ratified SOI: flight service specialists brief and file for pilots who call in, which is mainly general aviation. Part 121 carriers file through their own dispatch systems (see `faa2025jo72103ee` §6-5, the Air Carrier Computer Interface Program). The useful part for this capstone is narrow: **what a flight plan contains and how it reaches ATC.**

- **Literature review:** `to-do-list.md` §4, flight-plan filing only. Not a core flight-execution source.
- **Decomposition / architecture (§6, §10):**
  - **Flight plan as an information object.** Appendix A gives the content of the international-format flight plan (FAA Form 7233-4) in four groups: flight-specific (TBL A-1), aircraft-specific (TBL A-19), flight routing (TBL A-20), and supplementary (Item 19). TBL A-1 maps each item to its domestic requirement. This is a ready attribute structure for a `FlightPlan` item in the to-do §10 information-object model (ECD 10-29), and the flight plan is the third link in the trajectory-intent chain.
  - **What ATC actually receives.** "For IFR flight plans, all items except Item 19 are transmitted to the ARTCC as part of the IFR flight plan proposal. Item 19 information is retained by the service that filed the flight plan and made available to ATC upon request" (¶6-2-1c1). So the filing service and ATC hold different subsets of the same plan.
  - **Route to ATC.** Flight plans and amendments are transmitted "to the ARTCC for the departure point" (¶6-2-2a). TBL 6-2-1 gives a filer lockout time per ARTCC: 46 minutes at most centers, 55 at Boston, 61 at Indianapolis and New York, 43 at Anchorage. Plans are deleted automatically 90 to 240 minutes after proposed departure if not acted on.
  - **Format rule:** the international format is mandatory for any flight "requesting routing that requires performance based navigation" (¶6-2-1c5(c)), and for RNAV SID, STAR, or point-to-point assignment in domestic airspace (¶6-2-1c6). A Part 121 hub-to-hub flight on RNAV procedures therefore uses the 7233-4 content.
- **Stakeholder / objective ontology (§7-§9):** little. FSS is a candidate entry under Information Services (an absorbed capability per D-007), not a modeled system.
- **Optimization study (§11-§13):** none.
- **Glossary / terminology:** FPL and SPL message types, Item 18 indicators (OPR/, ORGN/, PBN/, DOF/, STS/), type of flight "S − Scheduled Air Service" (Appendix A, Item 8b), tie-in facility. The Pilot/Controller Glossary is bound in (PDF pp. 249–398).

## Rating

**3/5** — Useful background: the order is about flight service specialists, who are outside the Part 121 scope, but Appendix A and Section 6-2 are an authoritative description of flight plan content and its path to the ARTCC. Likely cited once, for the flight-plan information object.

## Flags

- **Edition: Basic only.** TBL 1-1-1 schedules Changes 1–3 for 2025-08-07, 2026-01-22, and 2026-07-09, and JO 7110.10FF for 2026-12-24. This file does not include those changes, unlike the AIM and JO 7210.3EE copies in the register. Check the FAA site for the consolidated edition before quoting paragraph text.
- **Overlap, not a duplicate:** the Pilot/Controller Glossary is also bound into `faa2025jo711065bb` and `faa2025aim` (the AIM copy is the newest). `faa2025aim` Appendix 4 has the same title as Appendix A here (FAA Form 7233-4); the two were not compared. If only one is cited for flight plan content, prefer whichever is consolidated to the current change.
- **Scope caution:** this order describes how an FSS handles a flight plan. It does not describe how an airline dispatch system files one. For Part 121, the operator → ARTCC filing path is in `faa2025jo72103ee` §6-5. Do not cite this order as evidence of airline filing practice.
- **Read depth is partial** (see below).
