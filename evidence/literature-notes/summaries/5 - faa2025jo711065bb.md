## What it is

The FAA's controlling document for air traffic control procedures and phraseology: it defines controller
positions and team responsibilities, clearances, separation, handoffs and coordination, and the
phraseology controllers use. Organized in chapters — 1 General, 2 General Control, 3 Airport Traffic
Control–Terminal, 4 IFR, 5 Radar, 6 Nonradar, 7 Visual, 8 Offshore/Oceanic, 9 Special Flights,
10 Emergencies, 11 Traffic Management Procedures, 12 Canadian Airspace, 13 Decision Support Tools —
plus Pilot/Controller Glossary. It governs ATC only; it does not cover flight-crew or airline dispatch
duties.

### Corrections to working assumptions (read these first)

1. **Appendix A and ¶2-1-24 are not the aircraft handoff.** "Transfer of Position Responsibility" is the
   **position relief** process between two *specialists* (shift change / relief at a sector or position) —
   a controller-to-controller briefing, nothing to do with the aircraft or pilot. Aircraft handoff lives in
   ¶2-1-14, ¶2-1-15, ¶2-1-17, ¶5-4-1..8, ¶4-7-9, ¶8-2-2 (below). The two should not share a line in the model.
2. **¶5-5-7 "passing or diverging" is mostly a controller rule, not aircraft-to-aircraft monitoring.** The controller
   applies it from radar observation. The only pilot role is the en route / STARS-multi-sensor variant (¶5-5-7b), where one
   pilot must *report* having seen the other aircraft and passed it. Any "aircraft-to-aircraft" awareness is relayed by
   the controller as traffic information.
3. **LUAW (¶3-9-4) is a controller → pilot clearance, not inter-aircraft communication.** Awareness between aircraft is
   created by the controller's traffic call ("traffic holding in position", "traffic … six mile final").
4. **Airport lighting (¶3-4-x) is a control flow, not only a visual flow:** the *controller operates* the remote lighting
   controls (ATC → airport equipment), then the lights signal the pilot visually (airport → pilot).
5. **¶8-1-6 (position reports) does not name the medium.** The medium comes from Ch. 13 §2 (ATOP): ADS-C/CPDLC, with HF/SATCOM voice as backup.
6. **"Sector eligibility" (¶5-13-6) is not defined in the order** — the paragraph only limits use of the automation's "OK" function.
7. **¶3-10-6 (anticipating separation) does not mention wake turbulence.** Wake turbulence is treated in ¶2-1-19, ¶3-9-5..7, ¶5-5-4g, ¶5-5-7.
8. **UTM in ¶2-2-11 means UAS Traffic Management**, not a flight-data acronym (P/CG: "UTM — see UAS Traffic Management").

### Communication channels used in the order (the vocabulary behind the tables)

| Channel | Used for |
|---|---|
| Voice radio (pilot ↔ controller) | Clearances, instructions, advisories, reports, readbacks; phraseology is prescribed |
| Interphone / landline (controller ↔ controller, facility ↔ facility) | Coordination, handoffs, point-outs, verbal verification of data |
| Automation (ERAM/EDST, STARS, ATOP, TBFM; Computer B network, NADIN) | Flight plan/amendment forwarding, automated handoff, AIT, data blocks, conflict probe, metering times |
| Flight progress strips (paper or electronic) | Posted control data and clearance status at a position; coordination indicator |
| Data link (CPDLC, ADS-C, AIDC) | Oceanic clearances, position reports, interfacility coordination between ATOP sectors |
| Broadcast (ATIS, "attention all aircraft") | Weather/runway/NOTAM info to all pilots on a frequency |
| Visual (airport lights; pointing at a display) | Runway/approach guidance; physical handoff by pointing at the target |
| Written agreement (letter of agreement (LOA), facility directive) | Overrides and specializes almost every procedure above; which exceptions apply is facility-specific |

the team as a whole is responsible for the sector/facility*, whether one, two or three people staff it.

### Chapter 6 — Nonradar

Nonradar minima are applied by the controller by **clearing pilots to times, fixes, and altitudes** (¶6-4-1: depart at a time, cross a fix at/before/after a time, hold until a time, change altitude at a time/fix); the pilot enacts them and reports position. Concise versions:

| ¶ | Minimum | Enacted by |
|---|---|---|
| 6-4-2 same / converging / crossing courses (longitudinal) | Lead aircraft ≥44 kt faster: **5 mi** (DME, or RNAV using ATD) or **3 min** (others); ≥22 kt faster: **10 mi** or **5 min**; (each only for the listed departure/en-route-at-same-fix cases). Climbing/descending through the other's altitude: 10 mi (descending lead/climbing follow) or 5 min with conditions (≤4,000 ft apart when the change starts; started within 10 min of the fix report); RNAV route ≤8 mi wide: 10 mi. | 6-5-4 other than established airways or routes | **Meaning:** direct courses / off-airway or random routes (e.g., direct-to, RNAV random routes) that have no published protected width, so the order defines the airspace to protect: NAVAID-based direct courses (course change ≤15°) 4 mi each side to 51 mi from the NAVAID, flaring at 4½° to 10 mi at 130 mi; degree-distance fixes 4 mi (below FL180) or 10 mi (FL180–FL600); larger course changes protect the overflown side; **GNSS-equipped RNAV on point-to-point routes:** 4 mi either side of centerline, points recallable from the nav database and displayed to the controller, ≤500 mi between points, altitude at/above the highest MIA | Controller |

**How RNAV/GNSS equipage changes things (your ¶6-5-5 question), from the text:** (1) protected airspace is defined by narrow, RNAV-specific widths (4 mi) rather than the wider airway/NAVAID cones; (2) distance-based longitudinal minima use ATD (along-track distance) for RNAV aircraft (¶6-4-2); (3) **GNSS-equipped** aircraft (/G /L /S /V) can be given point-to-point random routes and impromptu routes under nonradar separation, and are exempt from the radar-separation requirement that otherwise applies to RNAV aircraft on Q routes and random routes at/below FL450 (¶5-5-1 note); (4) ATC only "knows" the capability through the ICAO equipment codes → equipment suffix (¶2-3-8) that automation derives from the flight plan — so equipage is an aircraft/operator → flight plan → controller information flow.

### Chapter 13 §1 — ERAM/EDST (en route decision support)

*What it is (¶13-1-1):* EDST is an integrated function of ERAM used by the sector team for **strategic planning**. It uses the *current plan* (what the en route automation predicts the aircraft will fly, possibly including clearances not yet issued), forecast winds, aircraft performance characteristics, and track data to derive expected trajectories and **predict conflicts** between aircraft and between aircraft and special-use/designated airspace; it also provides trial planning and flight-data management.

| ¶ | Answer to the journal question |
|---|---|
| 13-1-2 Conflict detection and resolution | **What detects:** EDST conflict probe. **What defines a conflict:** a predicted loss of *approved radar separation* (note: the probe does not account for cases needing more than standard separation — non-standard formations, A380 — or where reduced separation is allowed, e.g., 3-mile airspace); aircraft-to-airspace alerts are the second type. **Who acts:** the sector team scans EDST for predicted aircraft-aircraft and aircraft-airspace alerts, evaluates the alert, acts "as early as practical" per duty priorities, and prioritizes alerts. **Resolution mechanisms:** direct routing, altitude changes, removal of a flight-direction constraint (inappropriate altitude for direction of flight), removal of a static restriction — weighing impact on surrounding sectors, complexity, flight efficiency and user preferences. ### Appendix A — position relief SOP (what it actually contains)

**Actors:** relieving specialist and specialist being relieved. **Channel:** Status Information Area(s) (manual or automatic displays), written notes, a checklist, and a face-to-face verbal briefing at the position. **Process:** (a) *preview* — relieving specialist reads the Status Information Area, observes equipment, traffic and communications, then signals the preview is done; (b) *verbal briefing* — the specialist being relieved briefs abnormal status, weather, traffic, and communications status of known aircraft (except at ERAM facilities using the Voice Communication Indicator); (c) *assumption* — relieving specialist states responsibility has been assumed, the other notes the time; (d) *review* — relieving specialist verifies, the relieved one stays to catch omissions, then signs on/off. **Responsibility:** the relieved specialist ensures pertinent status is displayed or relayed; the relieving specialist resolves open questions *before* accepting; both share equal responsibility for completeness. It moves nothing about an aircraft's control — it is continuity of *operator* responsibility, which matters if the capstone models "who holds control at time t".

### Candidate implications for the capstone (suggestions, not decisions)

- **Handoff vs. position relief vs. point-out** are three different events with different actors and different data; model them separately (Appendix A/¶2-1-24; ¶5-4 handoff/point-out; ¶8-2-2 oceanic unit-to-unit transfer).
- **Position-holder split** (R/RA/RC/FD/NR; LC/GC/CC/CD) is defined by the order but with an explicit "no absolute division" clause — supports a single ATC/controller-team actor in the context diagram with roles as attributes unless a specific interface needs a finer split (relates to the still-open Flight Crew/ATC split questions).
- **The EDCT / TMI chain supports pointing the AOC link at TFM, not the tactical `atc` part** (the 09-24 open question). The chain passes *through* ATC facilities — tower → TMU → ATCSCC (¶4-3-4 for EDCTs; ¶4-4-6 for protected-segment reroutes, controller → TMU → ATCSCC) — but what it carries is a flow constraint originating at the ATCSCC, not a separation decision; tower's role is to conform (depart within −5/+5 min, verify a reported EDCT with the TMU). So TFM is the constraint's source and tower/TMU are its conduit. **Evidence grade:** A for the tower/TMU/ATCSCC path, for the operator being "responsible for operating to meet the EDCT," and for EDCTs being revised by "air carriers and traffic management" (¶4-3-4). The AOC ↔ ATCSCC exchange itself is *not* described in this order (it's the controller's handbook) — that stays B/C until JO 7210.3 or FAA CDM/CTOP material is registered.
- **Apron/movement-area boundary** (¶3-7-2 note) and **the controller operating airport lighting** (¶3-4) are direct evidence for the ATC ↔ Airport link and its direction.
- **Equipment/CNS capability reaches ATC through the flight plan** (¶2-3-8, ¶2-1-29f) — an aircraft/operator → ATC information flow that also changes which separation rules apply (RVSM, RNAV/GNSS).
- **Conflict definition** used by the probe = *approved radar separation* (¶13-1-2), which is a usable single definition for the decision-support capability inside the architecture.
