- **DOI:** 10.1080/03081060701835738

## What it is

A primary System-of-Systems (SoS) study from Purdue's School of Aeronautics & Astronautics.
Applies an explicit SoS methodological approach, combined with network-topology analysis, to
understand future airport requirements under emerging distributed/on-demand air transportation
paradigms (very light jets, on-demand air service/"air taxi," personal air vehicles). Identifies
the airport's "adjacent systems" (terminal airspace, aircraft, ground transport, other airports,
the National Airspace System itself, local government/economy) and their influence, then
demonstrates network models (hub-and-spoke vs. point-to-point) using Indiana airports as a case
study to assess airport "fitness" (network centrality) under different service models.

## Why it's valuable — and to what

- Literature review section: `to-do-list.md` §2-§4 — SoS-framed background on airport-level
  systems within the broader air transportation network.
- Decomposition / architecture (§6, §10): directly relevant — Table 1 ("The influence of systems

  National Airspace System as one of the airport's adjacent systems, useful as a precedent for how
  to bound and decompose the airport node within a NAS-as-SoS architecture.
- Stakeholder / objective ontology (§7-§9): identifies airport stakeholder groups and their
  differing "influence" relationships (regulatory compliance, customer needs, economic drivers),
  useful for stakeholder/objective mapping at the airport level.
- Optimization study (§11-§13): the network-topology "fitness" framing (centrality-based measures
  of airport performance under different service models) is a candidate methodological analog for
  framing optimization objectives at a network level.
- Glossary / terminology: reinforces SoS vocabulary (heterogeneous/distributed/autonomously
  operating constituent systems) already established via `delaurentis2005sosTransportation`.

## Flags

`delaurentis2005sosTransportation` (AIAA 2005-123, "Understanding Transportation as a
System-of-Systems Design Problem"). That paper establishes the general SoS-for-transportation
taxonomy; this 2008 journal article is a distinct, more applied follow-on study specifically about
airports under distributed/on-demand aviation, co-authored with Donald N. Fry. Worth citing
together as a pair (general taxonomy -> airport-specific application) rather than treating either
as redundant.
