- **DOI:** none (FAA publication)

## What it is

An FAA advisory circular for pilots moving to aircraft that fly above 25,000 ft or faster than Mach 0.75. It is training guidance under 14 CFR 61.31, not a rule and not performance data for any type. Chapter 1 is definitions; chapter 2 is a recommended training syllabus (physiology, weather, flight planning, emergencies); chapter 3 is Mach flight (critical Mach, Mach tuck, buffet, coffin corner).

## Why it's valuable — and to what

- Literature review section: none.
- Decomposition / architecture (§6, §10): none.
- Stakeholder / objective ontology (§7-§9): none directly.
- Optimization study (§11-§13): it gives the definition the experiment now uses to decide whether an aircraft can be cleared to a level.
  - **Service ceiling:** "The maximum height above MSL at which an airplane can maintain a rate of climb of 100 ft per minute (ft/min) under normal conditions" (para. 1-4r, p. 2). Used in `projects/nas-sos-capstone/simulation/b739_envelope.py` and `experiment_scoring.py` (D-023).
  - **Coffin corner:** the upper part of the envelope "for a given gross weight and G-force" where the stall speed and the critical Mach close in, so that a disturbance such as clear air turbulence can push the aircraft outside its speed limits (para. 1-4q, p. 2; para. 3-2 item 5, p. 42). This is the published basis for saying a heavy aircraft high in its envelope has little room to manoeuvre.
  - **Margins shrink with altitude and weight:** "Increasing either gross weight or load factor (G factor) will increase the low-speed buffet and decrease Mach buffet speeds" (para. 3-2 item 6, p. 42); in "a typical jet aircraft" the buffet-free speed margin at 1.0 G falls from 135 kt at FL350 to 26 kt at FL450 (para. 3-3 item 5(b), p. 46). The second figure is an illustration for an unnamed type, not 737 data.
  - "A maximum cruising flight altitude must be selected that will allow sufficient buffet margin for the maneuvering necessary and for gust conditions likely to be encountered" (p. 42). This supports keeping a margin; it does not give a number.
- Glossary / terminology: service ceiling, coffin corner.
- Other: none.

## Rating

**3/5** — one definition the experiment depends on, from an FAA document, plus qualitative support for the envelope figure; nothing on air traffic control, airline operations or the 737.

## Flags

- **The 150 ft/min operator margin is not in this document.** The circular gives only the 100 ft/min definition. The 150 ft/min figure in `scenario_base.yaml` is the owner's industry knowledge [SME]; cite it that way or find an operator or manufacturer source.
- The definition does not say at what thrust rating, speed or weight the 100 ft/min is measured ("under normal conditions"). The scripts apply it at the aircraft's actual weight, ISA, with OpenAP's maximum thrust.
- The buffet figures (135 kt, 26 kt, 1.4 G at 51,000 ft) describe "a typical" jet and should not be quoted as B737-9 values.
- Not a duplicate of any held source.
