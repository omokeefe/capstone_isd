# Airbus Flight Operations Briefing Note: Aircraft Energy Management during Approach

- **File:** `evidence/sources/airbus-fobn-aircraft-energy-management-approach.pdf`
- **Bib key:** `airbus2005energyMgmtApproach`
- **Authors:** Airbus Customer Services, Flight Operations Support (corporate). PDF metadata names Christophe Lemozit.
- **Year:** 2005 (reference FLT_OPS – APPR – SEQ03 – REV02 – OCT. 2005)
- **Venue:** Airbus Flight Operations Briefing Notes, adapted from the Flight Safety Foundation's approach-and-landing accident reduction (ALAR) briefing notes
- **DOI:** none. URL: `https://skybrary.aero/sites/default/files/bookshelf/166.pdf` (SKYbrary bookshelf copy)

## What it is

An 11-page safety briefing for pilots on managing speed, height, configuration and thrust on approach. It asks "how fast can you fly down to the marker?" and gives typical deceleration figures and a worked example. The second half covers the thrust-required curve and engine response from idle.

## Why it's valuable — and to what

- Literature review section: none of §2–§4.
- Stakeholder / objective ontology (§7–§9): a documented conflict between a controller's wish for speed to the marker and the crew's need to be stable. "ATC requests for maintaining a high airspeed down to the marker (160 kt to 200 kt IAS typically) are frequent at high-density airports, to increase the aircraft landing rate" (p. 3).
- Optimization study (§11–§13): deceleration figures used in `knowledge/models/b737-max9-rules-of-thumb.md` §3.

## What was taken from it (2026-10-10)

- Level flight: "With approach flaps extended: 10 to 15 kt-per-nm; With landing gear down and flaps full: 20 to 30 kt-per-nm" (p. 3).
- On a 3 degree path: "With landing flaps and gear down: 10 to 20 kt per nm"; "Decelerating on a 3 degree glide path in clean configuration usually is not possible." (p. 3)
- Worked example at a "conservative deceleration rate of 10 kt per nm": 30 knots can be lost between the outer marker at 6 NM and the stabilization point at 3 NM, so 160 knots is the most that can be held to the marker for a 130-knot approach speed (p. 4).
- Statistics: about 70 percent of rushed and unstable approaches involve wrong energy management (p. 2).

## Flags

- **An Airbus document.** Its figures are "typical values" that "largely depend on the aircraft type and gross-weight". They are not 737 data and are not used as such.
- **Read depth:** full text extracted by script and read. The owner has not read it; no highlights.
- The document says the operator's manuals prevail over it.
