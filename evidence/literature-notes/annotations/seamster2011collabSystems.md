## What was extracted, and how

Tables 2.1–2.10, 3.1, A.1–A.2 (pilot survey items), C-1–C-3 (ATC interactions by controller position), D-1–D-3 (dispatcher survey items; D-4 is a blank write-in form) and **E-1–E-13, the interaction matrix** (report says "a little over 300 entries"; 277 interaction rows extracted from E, plus 6 "…" time-gap rows and 17 phase/sub-step/off-nominal header rows — every non-empty grid row on the 13 E pages is accounted for). 702 interaction rows in total. Page-token coverage against the source pages is 100.00% (see the script's `--verify`); E-7, 2.10 and 3.1 were compared row-by-row against rendered pages. **Not extracted:** future-state NextGen tables (Sections 4–5), the Appendix B process-chart figures (only the narrated handoff example, App. B p.6, is text), and §2.2 role narrative (summarized below, not transcribed).

Extraction notes that affect use: interaction IDs (`E-7#04`) are stable for a given script version; Table 3.1 is hand-transcribed from word positions (irregular row geometry) and flagged; a few subject/interacting-with group cells are blank in the source (E-7#16, E-12#16) and the group is inferred from the position and flagged; the report prints "Relephone" (Table 3.1) and "breaking actions" (Table 2.7/C-1) — kept as printed in `text_as_printed`; position labels are inconsistent in the source ("Last Center First Sector" vs "Last Center 1st Sector").

## Extraction

- **Actors / stakeholders:** four groups — flight deck (Captain, First Officer on the ground; Pilot Flying/Pilot Monitoring airborne), ATC, FOC, RAMP (added because load planner and pushback/ground personnel could not be placed under ATC or FOC; report p.44) — plus automation, which in the *current* matrix is allocated to a group, not treated as a collaborator (p.45). ATC positions: Tower CD/GC/LC/TMU; TRACON Departure R/RA, Approach R/RA/TMU, Final Approach R; En Route sector R/RA (one RC) by center and sector; Command Center TMU; also Supervisor and Flight Data (App. C). FOC: Flight Dispatcher and ATC Coordinator. Counts of each position in the matrix are in the interactions file.
- **Systems / organizations:** radio, phone/interphone/telcon/Satcom, ACARS (including fully automated OOOI reports), HOST (En Route flight-data system), PVD, URET, flight strips, ATIS, FMS, AFTN, Command Center and TMUs (pp. 43–44).
- **Objectives:** ATC — safe separation and efficient flow (p.20); FOC — company operations, with ATC Coordinators seeking to "push the company's agenda" with ATC in critical situations such as bad weather near a hub (p.34); flight deck and FOC "work for the same airline and have similar needs and objectives" (p.47–48).
- **Costs / penalties:** not quantified; delays, fuel cost and redispatch after diversion are named qualitatively (p.34).
- **Decisions:** clearances and control instructions (ATC), dispatch release (dispatcher + captain), reroute/hold/diversion/holding fuel decisions, EDCT/ground-delay/TMI decisions (Command Center/TMU).
- **Decision authority:** dispatcher and captain both sign the release (p.33); captain has "ultimate responsibility" in emergencies with dispatch (Table 2.2 #5); controller positions share "no absolute divisions of responsibilities" (team concept, JO 7110.65T ¶2-10, p.18); in CDM/ICR "responsibilities are not shared and the decision is ultimately made by Traffic Management" (p.46–48); clearance/control interactions are "procedural interactions where just one of the participants has ultimate responsibility" with "little room for negotiations" (p.46).
- **Activities / processes:** the generic SFO→JFK flight: flight planning before/after release, pushback, taxi-out, takeoff, cruise, descent, final approach, landing/taxi-in, plus off-nominal reroute (E-7), holding (E-9) and missed approach (E-12).
- **Resources:** flight data (plan, weights, ATIS code, EDCT, MEL, NOTAMs), strips, radio frequencies.
- **Information inputs / outputs:** the ~277 rows *are* the information exchanges (who → whom, medium, content). Examples: flight plan release to HOST; EDCT assignment; PDC by ACARS; load and passenger list; final weight and balance; handoff datablock via PVD; ATC coordinator ↔ Command Center weather/delay/runway-configuration advisories.
- **Constraints:** JO 7110.65T and 7210.3W (ATC), 14 CFR 91/121, AC 121-32A, FAA 8900.1, FAA S-8081-10C/12B; SOPs and letters of agreement between facilities (p.20); ACARS/CPDLC availability.
- **Interfaces / handoffs:** sector-to-sector handoff via PVD (simple: send/accept; complex: receiving sector asks feeding sector to relay instructions first; App. B p.6), point-outs, tower–TRACON departure release by phone, no handoff for approaching aircraft — assumed automatic ~7 miles before the runway once cleared for approach and told to contact Tower (p.18).
- **Timescales of decisions:** flight strips printed 30 min before operation (App. C); Command Center telcons every 2 hours 06:00–20:00 EST (p.22); dispatcher contact with each monitored flight at least every 2 hours (p.34); holding instruction at least 5 min before the fix (Table 2.8/C-3); everything else per the sequential matrix.
- **Upstream dependencies:** flight planning → release → PDC → pushback → …; flow constraints from the Command Center reach flights via TMUs, FOC ATC Coordinators and dispatchers.
- **Downstream consequences:** diversion means redispatch of passengers and cargo (p.34); rerouting propagates to load/fuel recalculation (see `munro2018managingVariability`).
- **Optimization variables:** none formalized.
- **Objective functions / measures of effectiveness:** none; the report's own measures are interaction frequency (VL/L/M/H) and criticality (L/M/H) ratings.
- **Evidence of local-vs-system-level conflicts:** (i) ATC Coordinators pushing the company agenda vs. Command Center flow decisions; (ii) FOC–ATC collaboration is "at a higher level" only (routes/delays) — tactical control stays with ATC; (iii) survey results: the report notes that none of the more collaborative flight-deck interactions was rated very frequent or high-criticality (p.29); the most critical off-nominal FOC↔flight-deck interactions involve fuel, performance limits, diversion and unauthorized-airport landings (Table 2.10). Candidate raw material for §9, small-sample.

## Confidence / limitations

Medium-high on the *extraction* (100% token coverage; every E-page grid row accounted for; three tables checked row-by-row visually); medium-low on *generalization*. It is a **draft** (unfilled documentation-page fields), from **2011** (NextGen-era; URET has since been replaced by EDST per JO 7110.65BB); the matrix is a **single constructed flight** (KSFO→KJFK) built from FlightAware logs, LiveATC monitoring and manuals, so it shows *a* sequence, not the range of practice; ATC content comes from **3 retired-controller SMEs**; ratings are **pilots n=11 from one operator** (avg 23 years, 13,500 hours) and **dispatchers n=4 at one domestic operator**, and the rated tables list only items above threshold (blank cell = below threshold, not "unrated"). The report cites JO 7110.65**T** (2010), not BB; the BB paragraph column is filled by keyword rules (`-bb-rules.csv`) and is a **candidate list**, not a row-by-row verification: "no rule matched" means only that no keyword rule fired, and flight-crew/FOC-initiated procedure lists (Tables 2.1–2.6, 2.9–2.10, A, D) are marked n/a because JO 7110.65 does not govern them. Rated survey tables should not be quantified further without the raw data.
