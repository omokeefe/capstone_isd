Regional FAA Traffic Management Units (TMUs), located at ARTCCs and major terminal facilities, send real-time operational data to the Air Traffic Control System Command Center (ATCSCC) to balance NAS capacity with flight demand. The exchange happens digitally through the National Traffic Management Log (NTML) and the Traffic Flow Management System (TFMS), and also through scheduled and ad-hoc telephone conferences (TELCONs).

Main categories of TMU → ATCSCC data:

1. **Constraints and system adverse impacts.** The TMU notifies the ATCSCC immediately of any local event or component change that alters capacity or disrupts the NAS:
   - Infrastructure outages: runway closures, airport equipment failures, computer/software glitches, telecommunication (TELCO) outages.
   - Critical facility changes: temporary NAVAID or radar shutdowns, procedural changes affecting major terminals.
   - Weather constraints: localized convective weather, low visibility, or wind changes that call for pre-coordinated Severe Weather Avoidance Plans (SWAP).
2. **Traffic Management Initiatives (TMIs).** Local restrictions and flow controls, specifically initiatives expected to cause a reportable delay of 15 minutes or more:
   - Ground Delay Programs (GDPs) and Ground Stops (GS): the TMU coordinates Expect Departure Clearance Times (EDCTs) and gives notification before, or immediately after, starting a local ground stop.
   - In-trail restrictions: submitting or updating Miles-in-Trail (MIT) or Minutes-in-Trail restrictions between adjacent ARTCC sectors.
   - Time-Based Metering (TBM): operational status of Time-Based Flow Management (TBFM) arrival metering.
3. **Airspace structure and flow-modeling data.** The TMU defines and shares the boundaries of problem areas so the ATCSCC can apply macroscopic flow fixes:
   - Flow Evaluation Areas (FEAs) and Flow Constrained Areas (FCAs): spatial and volume data outlining congestion or weather degradation that may need nationwide reroutes.
   - Reroute requests: proposed airborne or pre-departure route changes (e.g., Trajectory Options Sets, Route Amendment Dialog parameters) to avoid local constraints.
4. **Metrics, delays, and forecasting data.** Uploaded continuously for global situational awareness:
   - Delay reports: formal logs of aircraft delay metrics per FAA operational requirements.
   - Monitor Alert Parameter (MAP) alerts: "Red" and "Yellow" sector-loading triggers when a sector's or airport's expected volume exceeds capacity over a 1.5–2.5 hour look-ahead.
   - Agreements and directives: copies of all formal local interfacility traffic agreements and procedures.
