## 21:09 — Finding: cruise altitude/speed profile-following as its own workload source

Added a mechanism the earlier draft was missing: flights don't just fly one filed
cruise trajectory — dispatch paperwork commonly includes an advisory step-climb and/or
speed-schedule guideline (e.g., step to a higher altitude once below some gross weight)
that the crew may or may not actually pursue. Every pursuit is a discrete loop —
monitor gross weight, compare against the paperwork guidance, anticipate whether it's
worth it, decide to request or forgo, get clearance, fly it — and it costs *both* crew
and controller workload even when nothing goes wrong and no one is overloaded. Crews
may deliberately skip an available efficiency gain if the sector seems busy, which
erodes the fuel savings the flight plan assumed in a way that's invisible to the
dispatch-time cost calculation.

Folded this into [conops-hub-to-hub-trajectory-cost.md](../../../knowledge/models/conops-hub-to-hub-trajectory-cost.md)
as a restructured Phase C ("Cruise: Profile-Following and Tactical ATC Response") —
the ATC accept/modify/deny branch now applies per step-request rather than once for
the whole flight, and "workload" is called out as a cost dimension distinct from the
monetized generalized cost (fuel/emissions/wear/crew-$/passenger-time), tracked instead
as its own MOP (profile-compliance rate for the crew side, requests-processed for the
ATC side) per [[stakeholder-objective-ontology]]'s existing Workload/Sector-workload
rows. Also updated the [conops-scenarios.md](../../../knowledge/models/conops-scenarios.md)
scenario summary to mention it. New open question added to the CONOPS file's §5: how to
model the crew's request-or-forgo decision (deterministic rule vs. stochastic
compliance rate) — no literature source identified yet for real-world step-climb
compliance behavior.
