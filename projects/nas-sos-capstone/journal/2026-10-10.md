## Pilot rules of thumb for the 737 MAX 9 collected from the web

**What was worked on and why:** the owner asked for a web search for pilot rules of thumb for the 737 MAX 9 (knots that can be lost over a given descent on a 3 degree path, fuel per mile and similar), with sources cited and the result documented in the project.

**What changed** (not committed). All of it is AI-collected and not reviewed by the owner; no to-do boxes were checked and no decision was logged.

- New `knowledge/models/b737-max9-rules-of-thumb.md`: the rules in tables, each with its source, page, what aircraft it applies to and an evidence grade; the arithmetic done on top of the sources, marked as such; where sources disagree; the abstractions made and why; what was not found; five questions for the owner's own FMS experience.
- Six source files downloaded to `evidence/sources/`, each with a bib entry in `evidence/sources/references.bib`, a summary in `evidence/literature-notes/summaries/`, and a ledger row and flags in `evidence/source-register.md`: `faa2017iphCh3` (4), `britchford2021max89FuelBurn` (4), `roberson2007costIndex` (4), `airbus2005energyMgmtApproach` (3), `icao2026engineEmissionsDatabank` (3), `brady2026b737RulesOfThumb` (2). One web-only bib entry, `eurocontrol2026b39mPerformance`.

**Main findings:**

- Level-flight deceleration is about 10 knots per NM in three independent sources (FAA handbook, Airbus briefing note, b737.org.uk page).
- On a 3 degree path: 10 to 20 knots per NM with gear and landing flaps (Airbus "typical values"), about 5 knots per NM descending per the 737 page (condition not stated), and clean deceleration "usually is not possible".
- The only fuel numbers for the MAX 9 itself are flight-planning block fuel: about 12 to 15 lb per air mile and 4,000 to 4,800 lb per block hour, computed here from the Aircraft Commerce study's gallons.
- MAX 9 weights from the same study: empty 105,000 lb, maximum landing 163,900 lb, maximum take-off 194,700 lb.

**Things the owner should know:**

- **Most of the file is not MAX 9 data.** Only the Aircraft Commerce study, the ICAO engine databank row and the EUROCONTROL page are for the type. Section 1 of the new file says which is which.
- The Boeing 737 Flight Crew Training Manual, the proper source, is proprietary and was not found. Nothing is quoted from it.
- No MAX 9 cruise fuel flow and no MAX 9 optimum-altitude penalty table were found. The altitude penalty table in the file is for the 737-300 and 737-700, second-hand from a Boeing manual that was not seen.
- The EUROCONTROL figures were read through an automated page summary, not by eye.
- A rough illustration in section 11 suggests a 2,000 ft level change held for 100 NM costs on the order of 25 lb of fuel. If that holds, fuel alone may not separate the experiment's options. It uses a borrowed 737-700 percentage and is not a result.
- `experiment-options-and-scoring.md` was not edited. The MAX 9 weight bounds above could fill its unset heavy and light weights; that is the owner's call.
