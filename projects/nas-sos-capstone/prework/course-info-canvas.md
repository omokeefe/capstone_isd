Course Description
Welcome to your capstone experience! You have proposed a project in your field that has been accepted by your Program Director, and now we are excited to support you in this immersive experience. This experience provides you with an opportunity to apply your previous coursework to a project where you will demonstrate the potential for improvement over current state conditions whether in industry, the community, or on campus.

As the Course Coordinator for this course, I will oversee administratively your progress this semester. I’ll be checking in with you and your UM Faculty Adviser throughout the semester to monitor progress and identify any issues that might prevent you from successfully completing your capstone project.

While enrolled in this course, you will be treated as a consultant working on a project that must meet the expectations of the company and/or faculty sponsor. Your UM Faculty Adviser will help you navigate these relationships and help you meet these expectations. Your faculty adviser will work directly with you (or your team) to provide project guidance and approve any change in project scope toward fulfillment of requirements for academic credit.

Throughout your experience, you will build toward a final project report and summary that will be delivered to the project sponsor and your faculty adviser at the end of the semester. The course is graded as Satisfactory (S) or Unsatisfactory (U).

If you have any concerns or questions about your experience, please feel free to reach out to me as the course coordinator.

Course Minimum Requirements
Minimum Requirements for Approval of a Capstone Project Toward Academic Credit
Given the wide range of needs among different industry and faculty sponsors, we seek to be as flexible as possible regarding topic areas and project impact. Still, the project must fulfill minimum requirements to receive academic credit toward fulfillment of a degree. The following list provides the minimal requirements for a Capstone Project:

Be considered value-added to the project sponsor
Contains a metric (or set of quantifiable measurables) to summarize current state conditions which also may be used to show improvement or the potential for improvement with implementation of recommendations
Requires use of existing data and/or data collection; analysis, modeling, programming and/or design tasks
Of note, students are expected to either demonstrate an actual improvement over current state conditions or provide evidence and/or effective logic-based arguments of the potential for improvement if the project recommendations cannot be implemented within the academic term. This potential for improvement may be based on a pilot study/short term verification study, a simulation, or findings from the project.

Preferably, final projects shall include evidence of business and financial benefits to the sponsor (or the potential for value-added benefits). Students should work with their sponsor to quantify the benefits of their project where feasible and include implementation dates where applicable. Our preference is that these benefits are expressed on an annual basis in dollars (Note: Where possible, you should include a discussion of benefits in both your executive summary and financial benefits sections of the report).

Project Confidentiality
The University of Michigan recognizes that information contained in reports may be deemed sensitive or confidential. Students are responsible for working with their project sponsor (external sponsor) to determine what is allowable to include in a report to their UM Faculty Adviser. Make sure reports are approved internally, without confidential information, before submission. Students should never send any confidential information to UM without first obtaining approval from the sponsoring organization. (GM TEP students should never include confidential information and reference the TEP Policy on intellectual property and confidentiality).

In some cases, the University has an established agreement with companies regarding confidentiality. Still, the University prefers that you avoid including any confidential information in reports. For example, rather than using figures for actual cost savings, students may state improvement in percentages.  Of note, a final report with confidential information may be submitted separately to a company but not submitted to the University.  For the University, a ‘sanitized’ version of the report without confidential information should be submitted for archival unless otherwise approved. If a student obtains permission from the course coordinator to not submit a final report for confidentiality reasons, students must still provide a short summary statement (project synopsis) without any confidential information. If a report is deemed sensitive and/or confidential yet permissible by the company to send to the faculty adviser only, students should send the external sponsor approved report only to their faculty adviser (without uploading to Canvas).  Then, a separate email should be sent to the course coordinator without the report (with CC to their adviser) to alert the coordinator that the report has been submitted to the adviser only. Any questions at all about project confidentiality can be directed to the course coordinator for clarification.

Citing ChatGPT and AI
There is considerable discussion about how and when to cite large language models such as ChatGPT.  This discussion is ongoing and changing, so we recommend that you consult specific style guides, such as MLA, APA and Chicago, to get the most current guidelines.  We also highly recommend the Purdue University libraries' citation guide as it is extremely comprehensive and illustrative.   Here are quick examples for citing ChatGPT in MLA and APA citation styles:

MLA Format: "Description of chat" prompt.  Name of the AI tool, version of the AI tool, Company, Date of chat, URL.
MLA Example: "Examples of harm reduction initiatives" prompt. ChatGPT, 23 Mar. version, OpenAI.  4 Mar 2023, chat.openai.com/chat.
APA Format: Author. (Date). Name of tool (Version of tool) [Large language model]. URL.
APA Example: OpenAI. (2023). ChatGPT (Mar 14 version) [Large language model]. https://chat.openai.com/chat
IEEE is in the process of developing guidelines on citing ChatGPT use in their style. For now, use their "website" category:Template for Website citing: Author Firstname initial. Lastname. "Page Title." Website name. URL. Prompt. (accessed date)
IEEE Example: OpenAI. "Solid State Physics Explained." ChatGPT. https://chat.openai.com [Prompt: "What is solid state physics? Explain it to someone with no physics background."] (accessed August 2, 2023)
Currently JAMA style does not recognize citations to ChatGPT or other large language models.   JAMA style only recognizes content created by humans and exclude content created by artificial intelligence.  As this conversation around citing AI tools continues, this position may change so please consult JAMA styles directly. 

Questions to ask yourself
As GenAI poses to change the academic space and beyond, it is important for you to understand why and how you intend to use these new, powerful tools. These are a few questions to consider and note that the answers to these questions will vary for each person.

Is using a GenAI-based tool helping me learn more and think better?
Is using a GenAI-based tool enabling or hindering my mastery of the stated course objectives?
Is the content I generate accurate and verifiable? Is it free of biases that might harm other groups of society?
How will I treat content that might have been generated using a GenAI-based tool?
Is using a GenAI-based tool equitable to my peers in my course?
