\aidraft{Staying at the new level (D-041). Drafted 2026-11-07 at the owner's request; revised 2026-11-14 because Table~\ref{tab:outcomes} is now scored with the aircraft staying (owner's markup).}
An aircraft cleared to a level where it burns less fuel has no reason to go back to its planned level once the conflict has cleared, and its crew would likely ask to stay. Table~\ref{tab:level-check} makes that check for the option each case chose, and Table~\ref{tab:outcomes} is scored with its answer. In cases A and B the aircraft moved goes down 4,000~ft, to a level where it burns more fuel per mile, so it climbs back. In cases C and D, AC2 goes up 2,000~ft to FL400, where it burns 4.29~kg per nautical mile against 4.37 at FL380, so it stays. Staying changes two things. It removes the instruction to return, so cases C and D need one call instead of two, with the same fuel. And it leaves a benefit after the hold that Table~\ref{tab:outcomes} does not count. The experiment leaves that benefit out because it depends on how far the flight has left to go: counting it would tie the result to one route, and a flight close to its descent would see little of it. For this scenario's AC2, 584~NM from Denver, the rest of the flight is about 76 minutes, 61 of them after the hold, and staying at FL400 would save about 39~kg more, assuming constant mass and cruise all the way to the destination. In the similar-weights variation, case C's climb (AC1 to FL400) gives the same answer: it would stay, and save about 59~kg over the 125 minutes after the hold. Table~\ref{tab:outcomes} is therefore conservative for cases C and D.

Each column of Table~\ref{tab:outcomes} belongs to a part of the context diagram (Figure~\ref{fig:sysml-systemContext}). The aircraft column belongs to the aircraft and its flight crew. The airline column belongs to the airline operations center within flight operations. The sector column belongs to air traffic control within airspace management. The system column belongs to no single part. It is a property of the NAS as a whole, which is the reason no constituent system computes it today and the reason the model is needed to state it.

The exchange that cases C and D add is one interface in the model: the airline operations center's performance share to air traffic control's decision support, carrying the aircraft's performance and the flight's delay margin. It satisfies system requirement SLR-INF-02, which is tagged as part of the improved architecture because today's NAS does not meet it. The interface is not drawn on Figure~\ref{fig:sysml-systemContext}, which shows the baseline.

Reliability and resilience were set aside as objectives of the study (Section~\ref{sec:scope}) because a single choice between resolution options does not change them. Across many conflicts, more efficient resolution would serve them as a side benefit: a resolution that costs less time and fewer instructions leaves each flight closer to its plan and the sector with more margin for the next disruption. The experiment does not measure this.
